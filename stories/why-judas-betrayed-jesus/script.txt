Why would a man who walked with Jesus for three years
hand him over for the price of a slave?

Judas Iscariot carried the money bag for the twelve.
He was the one they trusted with it.

John chapter twelve, verse six calls him a thief,
who helped himself to what was in the bag.

So he went to the chief priests in the temple.
He asked one question.
"What will ye give me?"

They counted thirty pieces of silver into his hand.

Thirty was not a random number.
In Exodus, thirty pieces of silver is what a man paid
when his ox killed another man's slave.

That was the price they put on Jesus.

At the Passover meal, Jesus broke the bread and said:
"One of you shall betray me."

Judas ate at that table.
Then he walked out into the night.

In the olive grove at Gethsemane, he came back with torches and soldiers.
And he chose a kiss as the signal.

The Gospels name the silver.
Luke says Satan entered him.
They never hand us the whole of it.

But here is the part most people never hear.

When Judas saw that Jesus was condemned, he repented.
He carried the silver back into the temple.
He threw it down on the stone floor.
"I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood."

They would not take it back.

Zechariah had foretold it hundreds of years before.
Thirty pieces of silver, thrown to the potter,
in the house of the Lord.

That same week, Peter denied Jesus three times.
Both men broke.

Peter went back to the others and wept bitterly.
Judas went out alone.

The difference was not the size of the sin.
It was where each man carried it afterward.

One of them let himself be found.
